# Sinking Funds

What is a sinking fund? A sinking fund is money set aside gradually for a known future expense, so the cost does not become a surprise.

## Sinking fund vs. emergency fund

- Predictability: a sinking fund covers a known event and approximate cost; an emergency fund covers an unknown event and cost.
- Timeline: a sinking fund has a fixed date (for example Diwali or policy renewal); an emergency fund can be needed anytime.
- Examples: sinking funds cover annual health or vehicle insurance, school fees and festival shopping; an emergency fund covers job loss or a sudden hospital bill.
- Feeling: a sinking fund is planned, while an emergency fund is a stressful surprise.

## Formula

Monthly contribution = (Target Cost T - Current Balance B) / Months Remaining M.

Worked example for a vehicle insurance renewal: the target is a ₹8,000 premium, due 10 months from now, with ₹1,000 already saved. Monthly contribution = (8,000 - 1,000) / 10 = ₹700 per month.

## Common sinking fund categories in India

- Annual obligations: health/term insurance, vehicle insurance, school/college fees, property tax, annual app/software fees.
- Home and vehicle: vehicle service, tyres and repairs, appliance repair, home maintenance, society maintenance.
- Lifestyle and family: Diwali/festival shopping, weddings and family functions, annual trip or train tickets, birthday and gift budget.

## Implementation

1. List yearly bills: check the last 12 months of statements for non-monthly payments.
2. Keep money separate: use a separate savings account, an RD, or a virtual envelope. For goals more than a year away, a liquid or short-duration debt fund may fit; avoid equity for goals under 3 years.
3. Automate: set a monthly auto-debit on salary day for each fund.
