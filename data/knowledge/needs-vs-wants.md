# Needs vs. Wants

What is the difference between a need and a want? A need is spending required for housing, food, health, legal compliance, work and family responsibilities, while a want adds comfort, status, entertainment or speed but causes no structural harm if skipped.

## Defining the boundary

- Needs: spending required for housing, food, health, legal compliance, work and family responsibilities.
- Wants: spending that adds comfort, status, entertainment or speed, but whose absence does not harm your finances.

## Categorisation examples

- Housing: need = safe rented home or PG sized to your household; want = premium locality, larger flat than required, luxury amenities.
- Food: need = groceries, home cooking, a basic tiffin service; want = frequent Swiggy/Zomato orders, restaurants, premium snacks.
- Clothing: need = work and weather-appropriate clothes; want = branded fashion, sale-driven shopping.
- Transport: need = bus, metro, local train, or an existing two-wheeler; want = daily cab/Rapido/Ola rides, upgrading to a costlier vehicle.
- Utilities: need = electricity, water, LPG, basic mobile and broadband plan; want = top-tier fibre plan, multiple OTT bundles.
- Family: need = support for dependent parents, school fees, medical needs; want = costly gifts beyond your budget.
- Festivals: need = essential pooja items, modest family gifts; want = large shopping sprees and EMI-financed gadgets.

## Expense triage

If skipping an item would risk eviction, default, health problems or job loss, treat it as a need. Otherwise treat it as a want and apply the 72-hour rule: check again after 3 days, then buy only if it fits the Wants budget.

## The 72-hour impulse control rule

Sales, flash deals and "no-cost EMI" offers push impulsive buying.

1. For any non-essential purchase above ₹1,000, add it to a "wait list".
2. Note the price, date and why you want it.
3. Check again after 72 hours. Many urges fade, and you keep the cash.
4. Before buying on no-cost EMI, check the processing fee, GST on interest and whether the discount is lost.
