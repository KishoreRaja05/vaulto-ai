# Irregular Income

Irregular income means your earnings vary from month to month, so you need to separate when money arrives from when you spend it.

## The hill-and-valley problem

In high-earning months ("hills") spending tends to rise, and in lean months ("valleys") there is not enough cash. The fix is to keep all earnings in an income holding account, such as a separate savings account or sweep-in FD, and transfer a fixed "salary" to your main spending account on the 1st of every month.

## Step-by-step protocol

1. Find your baseline (B): the minimum monthly cost of rent/EMI, food, bills, insurance, debt minimums and family support. Example: ₹30,000.
2. Open a holding account: all earnings go here first.
3. Pay yourself a fixed salary: on the 1st, transfer B to your spending account. Using the lowest of your last 6-12 months' income is a safe guide.
4. Handle hills: surplus stays in the holding account until it covers 6-12 months of baseline expenses. After that, move extra money to SIPs, loan prepayment or goals.
5. Set aside tax: freelancers and business owners should keep a portion for taxes (TDS reconciliation, advance tax instalments due 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March, and GST if applicable). Confirm your own obligations with a CA.
6. Keep a larger emergency fund: 9-12 months of expenses is common for irregular earners.
