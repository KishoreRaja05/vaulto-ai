# Reducing Food Spending

How can you cut down what you spend on food? Food usually takes 15%-30% of a household budget, and the biggest leak is convenience, meaning paying for cooking, packaging and delivery.

## The food spending pyramid

From highest to lowest cost per meal:

- Level 4: delivery apps (Swiggy, Zomato, Zepto cafe, etc.).
- Level 3: restaurants and cafes.
- Level 2: ready-to-eat, packaged and frozen foods.
- Level 1: groceries cooked at home (atta, rice, dal, vegetables, eggs).

## Approximate cost per meal (per person)

- Home-cooked: ₹40 - ₹100, low convenience (needs time), no hidden costs.
- Batch meal prep: ₹50 - ₹110, high convenience after prep, low hidden costs.
- Basic tiffin service: ₹80 - ₹150, high convenience, low hidden costs.
- Restaurant dining: ₹300 - ₹600, high convenience, plus service charge and GST.
- Delivery app order: ₹350 - ₹800, maximum convenience, plus delivery fee, platform fee, packaging, GST, tip and higher menu prices.

Prices vary by city; use your own recent bills for a baseline.

## High-impact actions

1. Cut app orders to a weekly limit: ordering twice a week at ₹450 is about ₹3,600 a month, so reducing to once a week saves roughly ₹1,800.
2. Buy staples in bulk: atta, rice, dal and oil are cheaper per kg at wholesale shops, mandis and large supermarkets. Compare the price per kg, not the pack price.
3. Meal prep twice a week: cook dal, rice, chopped vegetables or curry bases in batches so busy weekdays don't end in takeout.
4. Order direct or pick up: many restaurants charge less than app menus when you call or collect.
5. Use membership plans only if you break even: delivery subscriptions help only when your monthly orders make up for the fee.
