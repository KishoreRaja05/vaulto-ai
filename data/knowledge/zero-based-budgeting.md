# Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB)

What is zero-based budgeting? Zero-based budgeting means giving every rupee of income a job so that income minus fixed expenses, variable expenses, sinking funds, debt payoff and investments equals ₹0.

## Fundamental equation

Net income - (Fixed Expenses + Variable Expenses + Sinking Funds + Debt Payoff + Investments) = ₹0.

Example for an in-hand salary of ₹50,000:

- Needs (rent, groceries, bills, commute): ₹25,000.
- Wants (eating out, OTT, shopping): ₹10,000.
- Sinking funds (insurance, festivals): ₹5,000.
- Extra debt prepayment: ₹5,000.
- SIP / investments: ₹5,000.
- Unallocated balance: ₹0.

## Core principles

1. Give every rupee a job: idle money in a savings account or UPI wallet is usually spent without noticing.
2. Plan on salary day: allocate at the start of the month, not at the end.
3. Flexible envelopes: if groceries go over by ₹500, take ₹500 from another envelope such as Eating Out. This is budget rebalancing.

## Comparison with the 50/30/20 rule

- Detail level: zero-based budgeting is very high (every category); the 50/30/20 rule is broad (3 buckets).
- Effort: zero-based budgeting needs higher weekly or monthly review; the 50/30/20 rule takes lower effort.
- Best for: zero-based budgeting suits tight budgets, debt payoff and people who overspend; the 50/30/20 rule suits simple tracking with higher or steady income.
- Goal: zero-based budgeting aims for full control of cash flow; the 50/30/20 rule aims for quick ratio guardrails.

## Step-by-step execution

1. List income: salary plus any freelance, rent or other regular income.
2. List fixed bills: rent, EMIs, insurance premiums, school fees, SIPs, mobile and broadband.
3. Estimate variable spend: use the last 90 days for groceries, fuel, eating out and shopping.
4. Fund goals: emergency fund, sinking funds (festivals, annual premiums) and SIPs.
5. Reconcile to zero: if money is left, send it to goals or debt. If you are short, trim variable envelopes first.
