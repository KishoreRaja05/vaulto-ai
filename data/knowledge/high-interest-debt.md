# High-Interest Debt

High-interest debt is borrowing whose interest rate is clearly above what safe investments can earn, roughly above 12%-15% a year.

## What counts as high-interest debt

- Credit card revolving balance: about 3%-4% a month (roughly 36%-48% a year).
- Instant loan apps and BNPL late fees: often higher; can be very costly.
- Unsecured personal loan: roughly 12%-24% a year, depending on profile.
- Home loan: usually much lower, so treat it differently.

The minimum due trap: paying only the "minimum due" on a credit card leaves the rest to accrue interest. Always pay the total amount due when possible.

## Payoff methods

Debt avalanche saves the most money. Pay minimums on all debts, then send extra money to the debt with the highest interest rate. It gives the lowest total interest and fastest payoff overall.

Debt snowball is best for motivation. Pay minimums on all debts, then send extra money to the smallest balance first. It gives quick wins as each small debt closes.

## Comparison

- Focus: avalanche maximises interest savings; snowball builds motivation and momentum.
- Order: avalanche targets the highest rate first; snowball targets the smallest balance first.
- Total interest: avalanche is lowest; snowball is slightly higher.
- Time to finish: avalanche is fastest; snowball is slightly longer.

## Execution steps

1. Pay at least the minimum on every debt on time (late fees and credit score damage are costly).
2. Choose the target debt (avalanche = highest rate, snowball = smallest balance).
3. Put all extra money into that target.
4. When it is cleared, move the full payment to the next target.
5. Repeat until all high-interest debt is gone.

Consider converting a large card balance to a lower-rate loan only after checking fees; stop new card spending while repaying; do not use one loan app to pay another.

## Loan prepayment vs investing

Floating-rate loans to individuals from banks generally have no prepayment charges (confirm with your lender). For rates above about 12%, repaying debt usually beats investing; for a low-rate home loan, investing alongside is common.
