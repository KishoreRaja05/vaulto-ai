# Savings Rate

What is a savings rate and how is it calculated? Your net savings rate is monthly savings, investments and extra loan prepayment divided by your net in-hand income, times 100.

## Core formulas

Net savings rate (%) = (Monthly Savings + Investments + Extra Loan Prepayment) / Net In-Hand Income x 100.

Gross savings rate (%) = (Personal Savings + Investments + Employer EPF/NPS Contribution) / (Gross Income + Employer Contribution) x 100.

Example: in-hand ₹60,000. SIP ₹10,000 + RD ₹2,000 + extra loan prepayment ₹3,000 = ₹15,000. Savings rate = 15,000 / 60,000 = 25%.

What counts: SIPs, stocks, RD/FD additions, PPF, NPS, VPF, emergency fund top-ups and extra loan principal. What does not count: minimum EMI and insurance premiums, because these are needs.

## Approximate years to financial independence

These figures are illustrative only. They assume about 7% inflation-adjusted returns and constant spending; real results vary with inflation, markets and healthcare costs.

- Savings rate 5%: about 66 years.
- Savings rate 15%: about 43 years (typical retirement track).
- Savings rate 25%: about 32 years.
- Savings rate 50%: about 17 years (accelerated FIRE track).
- Savings rate 70%: about 8.5 years.

## Benchmarks

- Below 0%: deficit, spending more than you earn, so debt is growing.
- 0% - 9%: vulnerable, with little cushion for shocks.
- 10% - 19%: standard, reasonable for retirement around 60.
- 20% - 35%: strong, with a good buffer and earlier freedom possible.
- Above 50%: aggressive (FIRE), with rapid wealth growth.

## How to increase it

1. Step up SIPs with raises: put at least half of each increment or bonus into SIP step-ups instead of lifestyle upgrades.
2. Cut the big three first: rent/EMI, transport and food usually matter more than small treats.
3. Automate on salary day: SIP and RD auto-debits should run within 1-2 days of salary credit ("pay yourself first").
4. Use tax-saving options wisely: EPF/VPF, PPF, NPS, ELSS and health insurance can help under the applicable tax regime. Check which regime you are in before assuming a benefit.
