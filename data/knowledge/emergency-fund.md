# Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside to cover unexpected and essential costs so that one bad event does not become a long-term financial setback.

## Strategic role

The emergency fund keeps one bad event from becoming a long-term financial setback.

- Avoid expensive debt: credit cards charge roughly 36-48% a year and instant loan apps are often worse.
- Protect investments: you won't need to redeem SIPs or stocks during a market fall.
- Peace of mind: less stress when jobs, health or family needs are uncertain.

An emergency fund does not replace health insurance or term insurance. Get adequate cover first, because one hospital bill can be larger than any emergency fund.

## Target reserve sizing

The emergency fund target is monthly essential expenses (needs, including EMIs) times M months. For example, essential expenses of ₹30,000 a month times 6 months equals ₹1,80,000.

- Dual-income household, stable jobs: 3-4 months, because both incomes are unlikely to stop together.
- Single-income salaried (no dependents): 6 months, because one income, EMIs and rent continue.
- Single income with dependents (parents/spouse/children): 6-9 months, because fixed obligations are higher.
- Freelancers, gig workers, business owners, commission earners: 9-12 months, because cash flow is irregular.
- Startup, contract or single high-risk industry job: 9-12 months, because re-employment can take longer.

## Where to keep it

Prioritise safety and quick access over returns. Never keep emergency money in stocks, crypto, small-cap funds or locked products.

- Tier 1: savings account or UPI-linked account, about 15%, instant access.
- Tier 2: sweep-in FD or bank FD, about 50%, same day to 1 day; check premature withdrawal rules.
- Tier 3: liquid mutual fund, about 35%, usually within 1 working day.

Avoid for emergency money: PPF (locked), NPS, ELSS (3-year lock-in), real estate and gold jewellery.

## Is it an emergency?

An expense qualifies when it is unexpected, absolutely necessary and urgent enough that it cannot wait for the next salary. If the answer to any of those is no, use a sinking fund or the monthly budget, defer the spending, or pay from next month's salary.

Examples that qualify: job loss, urgent medical expense not covered by insurance, essential vehicle or home repair. Not emergencies: sales, phone upgrades, festival shopping and trips.
